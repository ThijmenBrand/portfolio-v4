session
  Reopen your windows after the page is reloaded. A reload ends every
  program, like a reboot; session remembers which ones had a window, where it
  was, and whether it was maximized or tiled, and opens them again at login.

  It starts by itself from /etc/autostart. While it runs it looks at your
  desktop every couple of seconds and keeps ~/.config/session.json current.

  Rules:
    - A link with ?launch= names the apps to open, so nothing is restored.
    - Windows your own ~/.config/autostart opened that were not in the saved
      session are closed again: once a session is saved it wins, so a window
      you closed stays closed. Ones that were saved are kept, not duplicated.
    - With no saved session (a first visit, or after the file is deleted)
      autostart decides what opens. A saved session with no windows is still
      a session: everything stays closed.
    - Frames are pulled back onto the screen if it is smaller than before.
    - System programs (taskbar, launcher) are never saved: autostart owns them.
    - Logging out does not overwrite the saved session.

  To start fresh, delete ~/.config/session.json. To turn it off, remove its
  line from /etc/autostart.

  See also: wm, to arrange windows by hand.
